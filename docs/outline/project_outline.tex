\section{Use Case}
% ============================================================

This project aims to build a unified view of approved pharmaceutical drugs in the United
States, the European Union and Canada, enabling researchers and analysts to query regulatory
status, approval history, and product attributes across these markets from a single integrated data set. To achieve this, we combine data from three complementary sources.
Drugs@FDA is the FDA's official database of US-approved drugs. EMA Medicines is
the European Medicines Agency's register of EU-authorised medicines. The Health
Canada Drug Product Database (DPD) contains current and historical drug products from
Health Canada. The central entity is a medicine, represented by its brand name and active
substance or combination of active substances. Each integrated record includes the licence
holder, regulatory status, product class, ATC code, dosage form, route, strengths, regional
regulatory dates and generic, biosimilar or orphan flags.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection*{Drugs@FDA}
% ------------------------------------------------------------
Drugs@FDA contains 51,839 product rows across 29,377 applications, forming 8,175 normalized medicine entities. Among applications, 78.3\% are ANDAs, 20.1\% NDAs, and 1.7\% BLAs. Three attributes exceed 30\% missingness: \texttt{ApplPublicNotes} (99.99\%), \texttt{SubmissionsPublicNotes} (99.83\%), and \texttt{ReviewPriority} (41.73\%).

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection*{EMA Medicines}
% ------------------------------------------------------------
EMA Medicines contains 2,746 medicine rows, forming 2,620 normalized medicine entities, with no duplicate input rows. Human medicines account for 85.6\% of the records. By status, 68.5\% are authorised, 15.4\% withdrawn, and 10.4\% are withdrawn applications. Twelve attributes exceed 30\% missingness, mostly because veterinary fields do not apply to human medicines and regulatory-event dates apply only to particular statuses.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection*{Health Canada Drug Product Database (DPD)}
% ------------------------------------------------------------
The Health Canada DPD contains 58,137 product records across 12 tables linked by \texttt{DRUG\_CODE}. They are consolidated into 42,042 medicine rows, forming 41,636 normalized medicine entities. Human products account for 87.7\% of the records. By status, 66.5\% are cancelled and 23.0\% marketed. Notable missingness includes ATC code (21.47\%) and first marketed date (11.67\%).

\subsection{Integrated schema and overlap with input schemata}

The integrated schema contains 15 attributes (Table~\ref{tab:schema}); ten occur in at
least two sources. \texttt{activeSubstances} and \texttt{strengths} are list-valued, while
the remaining attributes are single-valued. Strengths are represented as numeric
value--unit pairs.

For consistent counting across the three sources, \texttt{medicine\_id} combines the
normalized \texttt{brandName} with the normalized \texttt{activeSubstances} list. Text is
converted to upper case, accents and dosage-strength expressions are removed, and
punctuation and whitespace are standardized. Multiple active substances are deduplicated
and sorted before the identifier is formed.

\subsection{Entity Count Requirement}
% ============================================================

Table~\ref{tab:datasets} shows that the entity-count requirement is satisfied. Let $F$, $E$
and $D$ denote the \texttt{medicine\_id} values in Drugs@FDA, EMA Medicines and the DPD.
The pairwise overlaps are $|F \cap E| = 428$, $|F \cap D| = 949$ and
$|E \cap D| = 594$. Their deduplicated union contains
$\left|(F \cap E) \cup (F \cap D) \cup (E \cap D)\right| = 1{,}407$ distinct medicines,
exceeding the required minimum of 1,000.
